# Tornado reference: what the photos say

67 photos (user-supplied, 2026-10-19; the same image is sometimes in two folders). Sorted by what they show best.
Used for the designed tornado's look in `src/com.stormlab.supercell/Runtime/Rendering/Resources/StormSkinMarch.compute`
(`tornadoField` and the tornado lighting in the march).

For how a tornado's look changes over its life (condensation lifting and returning, rain-wrap, rope-out, re-forming),
see `selden_20210524/NOTES.md`: the user's own 15-minute video of the Selden KS EF1, 24 May 2021.

## The funnel's surface (`close_texture/`, `laminar/`)

- **Smooth, never grooved.** In every photo the condensation surface is smooth at the scale of the funnel. None show
  vertical grooves or streaks. Where there is texture it is faint banding that winds round the funnel, nearly horizontal
  to about 30 degrees (laminar: Elie 2007, `laminar/images.jpg`, `lighting/images.jpg`, `close_texture/images.jpg`).
- **Shaded like a soft cylinder.** One side is brighter, toward the light, and the edges fade a little. That shading,
  not texture, is what makes a funnel look round (Bennington-area cones, `close_texture/IMG_04843.jpg`,
  `close_texture/images (5).jpg`).
- **Clean, slightly soft edges** on cones, stovepipes and ropes. Ragged, scuddy texture sits where the funnel meets the
  cloud (the flared lowering, concentric bands round its top: Gary SD, `close_texture/lne6t44g21kf1.jpg`) and at the
  edges of wedges.
- **The top flares smoothly**, like a trumpet, into a lowering 3-6 times the funnel's width. There is no step between
  funnel and cloud.
- **Ropes and elephant trunks are smooth, nearly uniform tubes** that snake (`close_texture/e16nF6SQbHzXCMTS.jpg`,
  `lighting/images (4).jpg`, `debris_soil/hq720.jpg`).

## Brightness and colour (`lighting/`)

- **Front-lit in direct sun: near white.** Much brighter than the cloud base above
  (`close_texture/1n7znagxqubg1.jpg`, `lighting/images (1).jpg`, `lighting/g1ah3q3g21kf1.jpg`). The white is sunlight:
  the user's rule is that a funnel is white only while it is actively sunlit. In the storm's shadow it is grey, lighter
  when the bright horizon is behind the viewer.
- **Backlit: mid grey**, still often lighter than the base, with a brighter rim (`lighting/images (7).jpg`,
  `lighting/30884738.jpg`).
- **Dark grey to near black** when loaded with debris or rain, or under a dark base: wedges, Andover 2022,
  `lighting/download.jpg`, `lighting/images (5).jpg`.
- **Low sun: peach to orange-tan** on the sunlit side (Traer IA, `lighting/maxresdefault.jpg`,
  `lighting/images (2).jpg`, `lighting/6bedc258...jpg`).

## Debris (`debris_soil/`)

- **Big and opaque.** The debris cloud is 2-6 funnel widths across and 100-400 m tall. It often wraps the whole lower
  third of the funnel in a dust sheath, which can hide the condensation down there (`debris_soil/16ec53dc...jpg`,
  `debris_soil/fb1e749a...jpg`, `debris_soil/images (3).jpg`, `debris_soil/still-...jpg`, Mexico dust tornado).
- **Colour follows the soil:** tan to red-brown on the Plains (`debris_soil/20210423-Tornado_near_Vernon_Texas.jpg`,
  `debris_soil/tornado.jpg`), dark grey-brown over wet Midwest fields (`debris_soil/118837-midwesttornado...jpg`),
  near black when full of vegetation and building debris (Manitou OK, Elie).
- **Ragged outer edge** with clumps and flying debris, and a smoother inner sheath hugging the funnel.

## Wedges and multiple vortices (`wedge_multivortex/`)

- **Wedges are dark, soft-edged masses**, often wider than they are tall, with ragged scud at their edges and horizontal
  motion streaks. There is no vertical structure (Bowdle SD 2010, Moore 1999, Bennington).
- **Multiple vortices are separate thin, translucent white tubes**, slanted and twisting round each other inside a
  dusty, wider circulation (`wedge_multivortex/27z73Swm4IAMd4Ff.jpg`, `wedge_multivortex/maxresdefault (2).jpg`).
- **Satellites** are thin ropes beside the main tornado (`close_texture/images (3).jpg`,
  `wedge_multivortex/maxresdefault (1).jpg`, `lighting/images (7).jpg`).
