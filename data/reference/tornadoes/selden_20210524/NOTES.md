## What it says for the designed tornado (StormGenome / tornadoField)

These are observations, not changes. Nothing in the code was edited.

1. **The condensation funnel and the vortex on the ground have separate lives.** In 15 minutes of one surveyed EF1,
   the funnel went cone, retracted, wide cone, hidden, rope, nothing for 2 minutes, thick cone for 30 s, nothing,
   while the vortex stayed on the ground. Only debris showed it was there. `TornadoPhase.cond` already exists, but
   a phase is a long segment. This needs condensation going up and down on a time scale of 30 s to 2 min, and a
   phase where debris shows but no funnel does (cond ~ 0, debris ~ 1).
2. **More than three looks in one weak tornado.** StormGenome gives a tornado at most pre/main/post phases. Selden
   showed at least six looks in the 15 minutes filmed, half its life. A weak, cyclic, rain-wrapped storm may need a
   chain of short phases rather than a single arc.
3. **A rope can be a stage, not the end.** The rope came 15 minutes into a 30-minute surveyed track. A new cone
   under a new, larger wall cloud followed 2.5 minutes later, and it was the **same tornado re-condensing** (the
   user confirmed this on 2026-10-22). The vortex stayed on the ground through the rope, the 2 minutes with no
   funnel, and the second cone. The survey's single track is right. For the sim: a rope-like look mid-life must not
   force the tornado to end. The vortex can thin to a rope, lose its funnel, then fatten into a cone again as the
   wall cloud reorganises above it.
4. **Rain-wrap comes from the RFD side** (left, south-west here) and hides the tornado from the bottom up. The debris
   near the ground stays visible longest, as a darker smudge inside the curtain.
5. **Backlit grey, never white.** Every stage is mid to dark grey against a brighter background, about as dark as the
   base, which fits the existing `lighting/` rule. The dust sheath is lighter and more translucent than the funnel.
   The late debris whirl is darker and more opaque than the funnel ever was, because it had picked up soil and
   wreckage near town.
6. **Proportions for a weak Plains cone:** slender stage about 6-8 to 1 (height to width at the top). The wide stage
   is a V whose top is about a third as wide as the cloud-base height, with debris filling the bottom third. The
   dust sheath is 2-6 funnel widths across, and the collar where funnel meets wall cloud is ragged and 3-5 funnel
   widths across. These agree with `../NOTES.md`.
7. **The wall cloud grows as the tornado cycles.** The second wall cloud (23:23-23:27) was much larger and lower
   than the first and filled the frame. The tornado under it was small by comparison.
